Letters you may get

  • Factura — your bill, usually every month or two
  • Requerimiento de pago / aviso de corte — unpaid bills; they may cut the supply
  • Cambio de condiciones — new prices or contract terms

What to do

  • If they warn about cutting your supply, call them right away and ask for a payment plan.
  • If your income is low, ask about the bono social. It lowers the bill and gives more protection from cuts.
  • Find the deadline. Look for “plazo”, “hasta el”, “días hábiles”.
  • Write the reference number from the letter on everything you send back, and keep copies.
  • If you disagree with a decision, object in writing before the deadline (“recurso de reposición”, “reclamación económico-administrativa”, “recurso de alzada”, “reclamación previa”, “alegaciones”, “oposición”, “recurso”). See the template.

How long do I have to answer a letter from the Compañía de luz, gas o agua?

It depends on the letter. Look for words like “plazo”, “hasta el”, “días hábiles” — they mark the deadline. In Spain, deadlines usually count from the day after the letter is delivered (notificación). A paper letter is delivered on the day you receive it; an electronic one when you open it, or 10 calendar days after it was made available. We estimate 3 days for post — use the real date if you know it. To object, the letter usually names the way to do it (“recurso de reposición”, “reclamación económico-administrativa”, “recurso de alzada”, “reclamación previa”, “alegaciones”, “oposición”, “recurso”).
