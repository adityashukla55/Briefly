Letters you may get

  • Disdetta — notice that your rental contract will end
  • Sollecito / Messa in mora — you owe rent and they ask you to pay
  • Rendiconto spese condominiali — the bill for shared building costs

What to do

  • Your rental contract must be registered with the Agenzia delle Entrate. Ask your landlord for proof of registration.
  • The deposit (deposito cauzionale) can be at most 3 months’ rent. You should get it back at the end, minus the cost of any damage.
  • Find the deadline. Look for “termine”, “entro il”, “scadenza”.
  • Write the reference number from the letter on everything you send back, and keep copies.
  • If you disagree with a decision, object in writing before the deadline (“ricorso”, “opposizione”, “istanza di autotutela”, “reclamo”). See the template.

Words you may see

Messa in mora
Formal demand to pay. After it, interest can be added and legal steps can follow.

Questions

What is the Locatore / Agenzia immobiliare?

Landlord or letting agency. The owner of your flat, or the agency or building manager (amministratore di condominio) that works for them.

How long do I have to answer a letter from the Locatore / Agenzia immobiliare?

It depends on the letter. Look for words like “termine”, “entro il”, “scadenza” — they mark the deadline. In Italy, deadlines usually run from the day the letter counts as delivered (notifica). For registered mail, that is the day you sign for it — or, if you don’t collect it, a set time after the post office leaves a notice. Our estimate adds 3 days to the date on the letter, so check the real date. To object, the letter usually names the way to do it (“ricorso”, “opposizione”, “istanza di autotutela”, “reclamo”).
