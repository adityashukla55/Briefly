Privacy

Your letter never leaves your device.

Last updated: 15 October 2026

The short version

  • Your letter is read, explained and translated inside your browser. It is never uploaded and never sent anywhere.
  • Nothing is saved unless you choose Keep this letter. Then the letter is encrypted with your password and stays only on this device.
  • No account, no cookies, no tracking, no ads, no analytics.
  • Like every website, Briefly is delivered by a hosting company (Cloudflare), which sees your IP address for a short time.
  • Only if you use Report a problem, the text you type is sent to me by email.

Who is responsible

Briefly is a free, non-commercial project by Aditya Shukla. To contact me about your data, use and add your email address so I can answer, or open an issue on GitHub.

Your letter

Text recognition (OCR), the explanation and the translation all run on your device. No server ever receives your letter. Nothing is saved unless you choose to keep a letter. If you don’t, close the page and the letter is gone.

How you can be sure: the page is locked by a security rule (Content Security Policy) that only allows connections to Briefly’s own website. It cannot send your letter anywhere else. The code is open source, so anyone can check it.

My letters (only if you keep a letter)

If you press Keep this letter, Briefly saves the letter in a private folder in your browser, on this device. It is never uploaded, and I can’t see it.

  • What is saved: the text of the letter, the country, what Briefly found (the kind of letter, the sender, dates, amounts and reference numbers), the day you kept it, your note, and whether you marked it as done.
  • Encrypted: all of it is locked with a password you choose (AES-256-GCM, with a key made from your password by PBKDF2 with 600,000 rounds). The password itself is never saved. Without it, nobody can open the letters — not even me — and nobody can reset it.
  • Locks by itself after 5 minutes without use, and when you close Briefly.
  • You decide: delete one letter or everything at any time in My letters. Clearing your browser’s site data also deletes the folder.
  • Backup file: only if you download one. It stays encrypted with your password, and you decide where to keep it.

Visiting the website (hosting)

Briefly is hosted on Cloudflare Pages by Cloudflare, Inc. (101 Townsend St, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA). When your browser loads a page, Cloudflare necessarily processes technical data: your IP address, the date and time, the page requested, and your browser and operating system. This is needed to deliver the website and protect it from attacks.

  • Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR — my legitimate interest in a working and secure website.
  • How long: Cloudflare keeps these logs only for a short time. I don’t receive them and don’t use them.
  • More in Cloudflare’s privacy policy.

Briefly loads nothing from other websites: no external fonts, scripts, maps or videos.

What is stored on your device

Briefly uses no cookies. Your browser stores only:

  • the country you picked last, and a note that you have used Briefly before (so the start page can be shorter);
  • the app itself, the text reader (about 6 MB) and, only if you use it, the translator for your language (20–26 MB), so Briefly works offline;
  • only if you keep a letter: your encrypted letter folder (see My letters).

This is needed for the features you use and is never sent to me or anyone else (in Germany: § 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). You can delete it at any time in your browser settings (“site data”) or by removing the installed app.

Reporting a problem

If you use Report a problem, Briefly sends:

  • what you type into the form;
  • your email address, only if you enter one;
  • the address of the page you are on and your browser name (for example “Chrome on Windows”);
  • under a result: what Briefly found — the kind of letter, the office and the country. Never the letter itself.

The form is handled by a small function on Cloudflare and delivered to my inbox as an email by Resend (Resend, Inc., USA). I use it only to fix problems and, if you gave your email, to answer you.

  • Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR — my legitimate interest in fixing mistakes and answering questions.
  • How long: I delete the email once the problem is solved, at the latest after 12 months. Resend keeps a copy for a short time to deliver it.
  • Spam protection: to stop bots from flooding the form, the function counts reports per sender. It stores only a scrambled (hashed) form of your IP address, never the address itself, and deletes it after about an hour.
  • More in Resend’s privacy policy.

Please don’t paste your letter or personal details into the form.

Data in the USA

Cloudflare and Resend are US companies, so data may be processed in the USA. These transfers are protected by the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and/or the EU Standard Contractual Clauses in their data processing agreements.

Links to other websites

Links such as “Buy me a coffee” and GitHub open other websites. Nothing is sent to them until you click, and then their own privacy policies apply.

Your rights

You have the right to access your data, to have it corrected or deleted, to restrict its use, to receive it in a common format, and to object to its use (Art. 15–18, 20 and 21 GDPR). Contact me as described above. You can also complain to a data protection authority, for example the one where you live (Art. 77 GDPR).

Because Briefly keeps no accounts and never receives your letter, the only data I can have about you is a feedback message you sent.
