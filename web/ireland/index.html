Official letters from Ireland, explained in English

In Ireland, many deadlines are short — for example 21 days to appeal a social welfare decision, or 28 days to pay a fixed charge notice. Many offices also send letters to your online account (MyWelfare, myAccount or MyGovID), so check them often.

Offices that write to you

  • Revenue (Revenue Commissioners) — Tax office. The Irish tax office. It handles income tax, USC, PRSI, Local Property Tax (LPT) and tax refunds.
  • Department of Social Protection (Intreo) — Social welfare office. Pays social welfare, for example Jobseeker’s Allowance, Child Benefit, pensions and illness benefit. Intreo is its local office.
  • County or City Council — Local council. Your local authority. It handles social housing, HAP, motor tax offices, parking and some local charges.
  • Courts Service (District / Circuit Court) — Court. A court. Court papers are always important — never ignore them.
  • Sheriff / Revenue Sheriff — Sheriff. Collects debts after a court judgment or for Revenue. Fees are added to the debt.
  • Debt collection agency — Debt collector. A company that collects debts for others, or that bought an old loan or bill. Check that the debt is yours and the amount is right.
  • An Garda Síochána — Police. The Irish police. It sends fixed charge notices for speeding and other road offences, and other police letters.
  • Motor Tax / NDLS — Motor tax and driving licences. Motor tax is paid online or at your council’s motor tax office. Driving licences are issued by the NDLS.
  • TV Licence (An Post) — TV licence office. An Post collects the TV licence fee. You need one if you have a TV set at home.
  • Immigration Service Delivery (Department of Justice) — Immigration office. Decides on visas and permission to stay, and issues the Irish Residence Permit (IRP).
  • Electricity or gas supplier — Energy supplier. The company that sells you electricity or gas, for example Electric Ireland, Bord Gáis Energy or SSE Airtricity.
  • Uisce Éireann (Irish Water) — Water company. The national water company. Most homes don’t pay for water, but there can be letters about excess use, leaks or works.
  • Landlord / letting agent — Landlord. The owner of your home, or the agency that manages it. Most tenancies must be registered with the RTB.
  • HSE — Health service. The Health Service Executive. It runs public hospitals and health services, and medical cards.

Common kinds of letters

  • Enforcement notice (sheriff) — Enforcement notice. A sheriff or solicitor will collect the debt. Fees are added, and goods can be seized.
  • Civil summons / civil bill — Court case against you. Someone is suing you. You must tell the court you will defend it, usually within a short time, or they can get judgment without a hearing.
  • Reminder / final demand — Payment reminder. A payment is late. If you don’t pay, the next step is often court or a debt collector.
  • Overpayment letter — Request to pay money back. They say they paid you too much social welfare or another payment, and want the money back.
  • Fixed charge notice — Road traffic fine. A fine for speeding or another road offence, often with penalty points. Pay within 28 days, or 50% more in the next 28 days. If you don’t pay, you get a court summons.
  • Parking fine — Parking fine. A fine for parking. Council fines usually go up if you pay late; private car park charges are bills under a contract.
  • Statement of Liability / Notice of assessment — Tax bill or tax result. Revenue worked out your tax for a year. It shows a refund, or tax you still owe.
  • Social welfare decision — Social welfare decision. A decision on your social welfare claim — for example the amount, a change, or that it stops.
  • Refusal letter — Refusal. Your application was refused. The letter says if you can ask for a review or appeal, and by when.
  • Approval letter — Approval. Your application was approved. Check that names, dates and amounts are right.
  • Meeting / appointment letter — Appointment. You must come to a meeting or appointment, for example at Intreo, a hospital or an immigration office. Missing it can reduce your payment or delay your case.
  • Request for information — Request for documents. They need papers or information before they can decide, or to keep paying you.
  • Notice of termination — Notice to end the tenancy. Your landlord wants to end the tenancy. The notice must follow strict rules — many are not valid.
  • Energy bill — Energy bill. A bill for electricity or gas. It shows what you used and when to pay.

Questions

How long do I have to answer an official letter in Ireland?

It depends on the letter. Look for words like “by”, “within”, “no later than” — they mark the deadline. In Ireland, many deadlines count from the date printed on the letter. When a deadline counts from when you received it, Briefly adds about 2 days for post — check the envelope or postmark if you are close to the deadline. To object, the letter usually names the way to do it (“review”, “appeal”, “dispute”, “objection”).
