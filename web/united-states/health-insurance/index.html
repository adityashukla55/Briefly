Letters you may get

  • Explanation of Benefits (EOB) — what your insurer paid; it is not a bill
  • Renewal notice — Medicaid or Marketplace coverage must be renewed by a set date
  • Denial of coverage — they won’t pay for a treatment; you can appeal
  • Premium bill — your monthly payment

What to do

  • An EOB is not a bill. Wait for the bill from the doctor or hospital, and compare the two.
  • Answer Medicaid renewal letters by the date given, or your coverage can end.
  • Find the deadline. Look for “respond by”, “pay by”, “no later than”, “within”, “deadline”.
  • Write the reference number from the letter on everything you send back, and keep copies.
  • If you disagree with a decision, object in writing before the deadline (“appeal”, “request for reconsideration”, “dispute”, “protest”, “hearing request”). See the template.

Words you may see

notice
An official letter. The date printed on it often starts the deadline.
appeal
A formal request to change a decision. It must reach them by the deadline.
EOB
Explanation of Benefits. A statement from your health insurer about what it paid. It is not a bill.
premium
Your regular payment for insurance, usually every month.
Medicaid
Free or low-cost health coverage from your state for people with low income.

Questions

What is the Health insurer / Medicaid / Marketplace?

Health insurance. Your health insurance company, or a public program like Medicaid or a Marketplace plan from HealthCare.gov or your state.

How long do I have to answer a letter from the Health insurer / Medicaid / Marketplace?

It depends on the letter. Look for words like “respond by”, “pay by”, “no later than”, “within”, “deadline” — they mark the deadline. In the US, many deadlines count from the date printed on the notice, not from the day it arrives. Some offices add a few days for mail (Social Security assumes 5 days), and court deadlines usually start on the day you were served. To object, the letter usually names the way to do it (“appeal”, “request for reconsideration”, “dispute”, “protest”, “hearing request”).
