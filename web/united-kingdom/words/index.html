English office words from the United Kingdom, explained

The words you see in official letters in the United Kingdom, in plain English.

acknowledgment of service
A form that tells the court you got the claim and will defend it. It gives you 28 days in total to send your defence.
administrative review
Asking the Home Office to check a refusal for mistakes. Often allowed only within 14 days.
arrears
Money you owe because regular payments were missed, for example rent or council tax.
CCJ
County Court Judgment. A court order that you must pay a debt. It stays on your credit file for 6 years.
charge certificate
Sent when a PCN wasn’t paid or challenged in time. The fine goes up by 50%.
claim form
The court paper that starts a case against you for money.
council tax
A yearly tax for your home, paid to your local council. The amount depends on the band of your home.
council tax band
The group (A to H, or A to I in Wales) your home is in, based on its value. It sets your council tax.
Council Tax Reduction
Help from your council to pay council tax if your income is low.
default judgment
A judgment made against you because you didn’t answer a claim in time.
deposit protection
In England and Wales, landlords must put your deposit in a government-approved scheme and give you the details.
Direct Debit
Permission for a company to take payments from your bank account. Protected by the Direct Debit Guarantee.
enforcement agent
A bailiff. Someone allowed to collect a debt after a court order, and who can add fees.
eVisa
Your digital proof of immigration status, in your UKVI account. It replaced most physical cards (BRPs).
final demand
The last reminder before they take further action, for example court or a debt collector.
fixed penalty
A set fine for a driving offence, often with penalty points on your licence.
formal representations
A written challenge to a PCN after a Notice to Owner. If refused, you can appeal to an independent tribunal.
HMRC
HM Revenue and Customs. The UK tax office.
journal
The message area in your Universal Credit online account. Many messages and appointments appear there.
letter of claim
A warning letter before a court case. For debts you usually have 30 days to reply.
liability order
A court order that lets the council collect unpaid council tax, for example through bailiffs or from your wages or benefits.
mandatory reconsideration
Asking the DWP or HMRC to look at a benefit decision again. You must do this before you can appeal to a tribunal.
National Insurance number
Your personal number for tax and benefits, like QQ 12 34 56 C.
NIP
Notice of Intended Prosecution. A letter that a vehicle was caught speeding or breaking a traffic rule.
notice of enforcement
The first letter from bailiffs. You have at least 7 clear days to pay before they can visit.
notice seeking possession
A notice from your landlord that they want you to leave and may go to court.
Notice to Owner
The next letter after an unpaid PCN, sent to the registered keeper. You can make formal representations within 28 days.
ombudsman
A free, independent service that looks at complaints you couldn’t solve with the company or office.
overpayment
Money paid to you by mistake that they want back.
P800
A tax calculation letter. It says if you paid too much or too little tax through your job or pension.
Parking Charge Notice
A bill from a private parking company, not a fine from the council or police.
PAYE
Pay As You Earn. Tax and National Insurance taken from your pay or pension by your employer.
PCN
Penalty Charge Notice. A fine from a council (or Transport for London) for parking or traffic rules.
PIP
Personal Independence Payment. Money for extra costs if you have a long-term illness or disability.
POPLA
Parking on Private Land Appeals. A free, independent appeal service for many private parking charges.
sanction
A cut in your benefit, for example because you missed an appointment without a good reason.
Section 172 notice
The part of a speeding letter that asks the keeper to say who was driving.
Self Assessment
The yearly tax return some people must send to HMRC, for example the self-employed. The online deadline is usually 31 January.
Simple Assessment
A tax bill that HMRC works out for you, without a tax return. You can query it within 60 days.
Single Justice Procedure
A quick way for a magistrate to decide minor offences on paper, without a hearing.
single person discount
25% off council tax if you are the only adult living in your home.
SORN
Statutory Off Road Notification. Tells the DVLA your vehicle is off the road, so you don’t need to tax it.
speed awareness course
A short course you can sometimes take instead of getting penalty points for minor speeding.
statute-barred
A debt too old to take to court — usually 6 years after the last payment or written acknowledgement (5 years in Scotland).
tax code
A code like 1257L. Your employer or pension uses it to work out how much tax to take from your pay.
Time to Pay arrangement
A payment plan with HMRC to pay tax you owe in monthly parts.
TV Licence
A yearly licence you need to watch or record live TV on any channel, or to use BBC iPlayer.
Universal Credit
The main monthly benefit for people of working age with low income or out of work. It is managed in an online account with a journal.
UTR
Unique Taxpayer Reference. A 10-digit number HMRC gives you for Self Assessment.
V5C
The vehicle logbook. It shows who the registered keeper of a vehicle is.
work coach
Your contact person at the Jobcentre for Universal Credit and job search.
