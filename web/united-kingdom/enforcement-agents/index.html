Letters you may get

  • Notice of enforcement — you have at least 7 clear days to pay before they can visit
  • Letter after a visit — what they took, or the fees they added
  • Controlled goods agreement — a payment plan where your goods are listed as security

What to do

  • Act fast: fees go up at each stage. Call them, offer what you can pay and ask for a plan. Get free debt advice the same day.
  • Bailiffs usually can’t force their way into your home for these debts. You don’t have to let them in — but don’t ignore them.
  • Find the deadline. Look for “by”, “within”, “no later than”.
  • Write the reference number from the letter on everything you send back, and keep copies.
  • If you disagree with a decision, object in writing before the deadline (“mandatory reconsideration”, “administrative review”, “representations”, “challenge”, “appeal”, “dispute”, “review”). See the template.

Words you may see

council tax
A yearly tax for your home, paid to your local council. The amount depends on the band of your home.
enforcement agent
A bailiff. Someone allowed to collect a debt after a court order, and who can add fees.
notice of enforcement
The first letter from bailiffs. You have at least 7 clear days to pay before they can visit.
CCJ
County Court Judgment. A court order that you must pay a debt. It stays on your credit file for 6 years.

Questions

What is the Enforcement agents (bailiffs)?

Bailiffs. Collect debts after a court order, for example for council tax, parking fines or a CCJ. They can add fees and, in some cases, take goods.

How long do I have to answer a letter from the Enforcement agents (bailiffs)?

It depends on the letter. Look for words like “by”, “within”, “no later than” — they mark the deadline. In the UK, a letter sent by first class post is usually treated as delivered on the second working day after it was posted. But many deadlines, for example for appeals, count from the date printed on the letter. To object, the letter usually names the way to do it (“mandatory reconsideration”, “administrative review”, “representations”, “challenge”, “appeal”, “dispute”, “review”).
