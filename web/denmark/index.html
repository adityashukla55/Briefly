Official letters from Denmark, explained in English

Most Danish offices send letters to your Digital Post (in the Digital Post app, borger.dk or e-Boks) instead of by paper, so check it often. Many deadlines, for example 4 weeks to complain, count from the day you received the decision.

Offices that write to you

  • Skattestyrelsen (Skat) — Tax office. The Danish tax office. It sends your yearly tax statement and handles tax on your income, car and home.
  • Gældsstyrelsen — Public debt collection agency. Collects debts you owe to public bodies, for example unpaid tax, fines, child support or fees.
  • Udbetaling Danmark — Benefits office. Pays many benefits, for example child benefit (børne- og ungeydelse), housing benefit (boligstøtte), state pension and maternity benefit.
  • Kommunen (Borgerservice) — Municipality. Your local municipality. It handles cash benefits (kontanthjælp), childcare, schools, local taxes and many social services.
  • Jobcenter — Job centre. The local job centre. It supports your job search and holds meetings if you get unemployment or cash benefits.
  • A-kasse — Unemployment fund. The unemployment insurance fund that pays unemployment benefit (dagpenge).
  • SIRI / Udlændingestyrelsen — Immigration offices. Decide on residence and work permits (SIRI) and on family reunification, asylum and other residence (Udlændingestyrelsen).
  • Politiet — Police. Sends fines (bødeforelæg), for example for speeding caught by a speed camera.
  • Byret / Fogedretten — Court. A court. The city court (byret) handles civil and criminal cases; the bailiff’s court (fogedretten) handles enforcement of debts and evictions.
  • Inkassobureau — Debt collector. A company that collects unpaid bills for others. Fees for reminders and collection are limited by law.
  • Udlejer — Landlord. The owner of your rented home, or the company that manages it.
  • El-, varme- og vandforsyning — Energy and water supplier. The companies that supply electricity, district heating and water.
  • Regionen (sundhed) — Health care. The regions run hospitals and send appointments for examinations and treatment.
  • SU (Uddannelses- og Forskningsstyrelsen) — Student grants office. Pays student grants and loans (SU) and collects repayment if you got too much.

Common kinds of letters

  • Lønindeholdelse / Udlæg — Seizure for a debt. Part of your salary or benefits will be taken, or your belongings can be seized (udlæg), to pay a debt.
  • Betalingspåkrav — Payment order from the court. Someone asked the court to make you pay. If you don’t object within 14 days, it can be enforced like a judgment.
  • Stævning / Indkaldelse til retsmøde — Court case. Someone is taking you to court, or you are called to a court hearing.
  • Rykker / Inkassomeddelelse — Payment reminder. A payment is late. Reminder fees and collection costs can be added.
  • Påkrav — Final demand for rent. Your landlord demands unpaid rent. If you don’t pay within the deadline, you can lose your home.
  • Krav om tilbagebetaling — Request to pay money back. They say you got too much money, for example housing benefit, child benefit or SU, and want it back.
  • Bødeforelæg — Fine. A fine, for example for speeding. You can accept it and pay, or refuse — then the case can go to court.
  • Årsopgørelse / Restskat — Yearly tax statement. Your tax for the year. It shows if you get money back (overskydende skat) or must pay (restskat).
  • Partshøring — Chance to give your side. They plan a decision that affects you and invite you to comment first. Your answer can change the decision.
  • Afslag — Refusal. Your application was refused. The letter says how and by when you can complain.
  • Bevilling — Approval. Your application was approved. Check that the amounts and dates are right.
  • Indkaldelse — Appointment. They want you to come to a meeting or appointment. Missing it can cut your benefits or delay your case.
  • Anmodning om oplysninger — Request for documents. They need more information or papers before they can decide.
  • Opsigelse — Notice to end a contract. Someone ends a contract with you — for example your landlord ends your rental contract.
  • El-, varme- eller vandregning — Energy or water bill. A bill for electricity, heating or water.

Questions

How long do I have to answer an official letter in Denmark?

It depends on the letter. Look for words like “senest”, “inden”, “frist” — they mark the deadline. In Denmark, a letter in Digital Post usually counts as received when it arrives in your mailbox. Paper letters take a few days. Briefly adds about 2 days — check the date in Digital Post or on the envelope. To object, the letter usually names the way to do it (“klage”, “indsigelse”, “anke”).
