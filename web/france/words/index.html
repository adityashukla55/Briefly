French office words from France, explained

The words you see in official letters in France, in plain English.

actualisation
The monthly update you send to France Travail to keep getting paid.
aide juridictionnelle
Legal aid. The state pays all or part of your lawyer if your income is low.
allocataire
Person who gets benefits from the CAF. Your numéro allocataire is your CAF number.
amende
Fine.
attestation
Certificate or written statement, for example proof of health cover.
avis d’imposition
Tax notice. It shows how much tax you owe or get back.
avis de contravention
Notice of a traffic or parking offence, with the fine to pay.
charges locatives
Service charges paid with the rent, like water, heating or cleaning of shared areas.
commandement de payer
Formal order to pay, delivered by a bailiff. It often comes just before a seizure or a court case.
commissaire de justice
Bailiff. A legal officer who delivers court papers and can seize money or goods. Until 2022 called huissier.
congé
In housing letters: notice to end the lease. In work letters: time off.
contestation
Challenge. Saying in writing that a decision or a fine is wrong.
convocation
Official invitation to an appointment. You are expected to come.
cotisations
Contributions you pay, for example for social security or an insurance.
CSG
Social contributions (CSG and CRDS) taken from most kinds of income.
date limite de paiement
The last day to pay.
délai
Time limit or deadline.
dépôt de garantie
Rental deposit.
échéancier
Payment plan or payment schedule. It shows how much to pay and when.
état des lieux
Written check of the flat’s condition when you move in and when you move out.
forfait post-stationnement
Parking charge from a town when you didn’t pay for parking. It is not a fine.
France Services
Free local help desks. Staff help you with the CAF, the tax office, France Travail and other offices.
indu
Money paid to you by mistake that you have to pay back.
justificatif de domicile
Proof of address, for example a recent energy bill or a rent receipt.
lettre recommandée avec accusé de réception
Registered letter with proof of delivery. Use it for important replies.
majoration
Extra charge added because you paid or acted late.
mise en demeure
Formal demand. A last warning before legal action.
notification
Official delivery of a decision. Deadlines often start from this day.
numéro fiscal
Your personal 13-digit tax number. It never changes.
OQTF
Order to leave France (obligation de quitter le territoire français).
pièces justificatives
Supporting documents, for example payslips, contracts or bills.
préavis
Notice period. The time between giving notice and the end of the contract.
prélèvement
Direct debit. Money taken from your account automatically.
prélèvement à la source
Income tax taken directly from your salary or pension every month.
quittance de loyer
Rent receipt.
RAPO
Required first appeal (recours administratif préalable obligatoire). You must send it to the office before you can go to court.
récépissé
Paper proving that you applied. It usually lets you stay legally while they decide.
réclamation
Complaint or formal request to change something, for example your tax.
recours amiable
A first appeal to the CAF, Assurance Maladie or URSSAF. It goes to their appeals board (Commission de recours amiable, CRA).
recours gracieux
A request to the same office to change its decision.
relance
Reminder. You have not paid or answered yet.
remise de dette
Cancelling all or part of a debt because you can’t pay it.
résiliation
Ending a contract, for example a lease, an insurance or a phone contract.
RIB
Your bank details on one page (IBAN and BIC). Offices often ask for it to pay you.
saisie
Seizure of money or goods to pay a debt.
taxe foncière
Yearly local tax paid by people who own a property.
titre de séjour
Residence permit.
trop-perçu
Overpayment. Money you got but were not entitled to.
virement
Bank transfer.
voies et délais de recours
The part of the letter that explains how and by when you can challenge the decision.
