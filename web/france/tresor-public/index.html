Letters you may get

  • Avis d’amende forfaitaire majorée — an unpaid fine that has gone up
  • Avis des sommes à payer — a bill from a public service, for example a hospital or school canteen
  • Saisie administrative à tiers détenteur (SATD) — they take money from your bank account or wages

What to do

  • Pay fines only on amendes.gouv.fr or as shown on the notice. Other websites may be scams.
  • If you can’t pay, contact them quickly and ask for a payment plan (délais de paiement).
  • Find the deadline. Look for “délai”, “avant le”, “au plus tard”.
  • Write the reference number from the letter on everything you send back, and keep copies.
  • If you disagree with a decision, object in writing before the deadline (“recours gracieux”, “recours administratif préalable obligatoire”, “recours amiable”, “réclamation”, “contestation”, “recours”). See the template.

Words you may see

majoration
Extra charge added because you paid or acted late.

Time limit or deadline.

Payment plan or payment schedule. It shows how much to pay and when.

Seizure of money or goods to pay a debt.

Fine.

Questions

What is the Trésor public / Service de gestion comptable?

Public treasury (fines and public bills). Part of the public finance office. It collects increased fines and bills from public services, like hospitals or school canteens.

How long do I have to answer a letter from the Trésor public / Service de gestion comptable?

It depends on the letter. Look for words like “délai”, “avant le”, “au plus tard” — they mark the deadline. In France, a deadline usually starts on the day you receive the letter — for a registered letter, often the day the post first tried to deliver it. The 3 days are only an estimate, so check the envelope or the delivery notice. To object, the letter usually names the way to do it (“recours gracieux”, “recours administratif préalable obligatoire”, “recours amiable”, “réclamation”, “contestation”, “recours”).
