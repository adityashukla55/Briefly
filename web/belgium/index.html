Offices that write to you

  • FOD Financiën / SPF Finances — Federal tax office. The federal tax office. It handles personal income tax, and in Wallonia also the yearly property tax.
  • Vlaamse Belastingdienst / Brussel Fiscaliteit / SPW Fiscalité — Regional tax office. The tax office of your region. It handles regional taxes such as property tax (onroerende voorheffing / précompte immobilier) in Flanders and Brussels, inheritance and registration duties.
  • Gemeente / Commune — Town hall. Your local town hall. It handles your registration (domicile), identity card, residence cards for foreigners, local taxes and local fines.
  • OCMW / CPAS — Social welfare office. The local public social welfare centre. It gives the living wage (leefloon / revenu d’intégration), emergency help and debt help.
  • RVA / ONEM — Unemployment office. The national employment office. It decides on unemployment benefits. The money is usually paid by your union or by the HVW / CAPAC.
  • VDAB / Actiris / Le Forem — Job centre. The regional job service: VDAB in Flanders, Actiris in Brussels, Le Forem in Wallonia. It helps you find work and checks your job search.
  • Ziekenfonds / Mutualité — Health insurance fund. Your health insurance fund. It pays back part of medical costs and pays sickness benefits when you can’t work.
  • Groeipakket / Allocations familiales — Child benefit office. Pays child benefit. In Flanders it is called Groeipakket, in Brussels Famiris, in Wallonia it is paid by the family allowance funds.
  • Dienst Vreemdelingenzaken / Office des Étrangers — Immigration office. The federal immigration office. It decides on residence rights, visas and orders to leave the country.
  • Gerechtsdeurwaarder / Huissier de justice — Bailiff. A legal officer who delivers court papers and collects debts after a judgment. He or she can also start amicable debt collection before court.
  • Vredegerecht / Rechtbank — Justice de paix / Tribunal — Court. A court. The justice of the peace (vredegerecht / justice de paix) handles rent, small debts and neighbour problems.
  • Politie / Police — Parket / Parquet — Police and traffic fines. Sends traffic fines and other police letters. Unpaid fines go to the public prosecutor (parket / parquet).
  • Incassobureau / Bureau de recouvrement — Debt collector. A company that collects debts for others. In Belgium it must be registered and may not charge you extra costs that aren’t in your contract.
  • Energieleverancier / Fournisseur d’énergie — Energy supplier. The company that sells you electricity or gas, for example Engie, Luminus or TotalEnergies.
  • Verhuurder / Bailleur — Landlord. The owner of your home, or the agency that manages it.

Common kinds of letters

  • Bevel tot betalen / beslag — Commandement de payer / saisie — Enforcement by a bailiff. A bailiff (gerechtsdeurwaarder / huissier) will collect a debt after a judgment or tax bill. Goods or money can be seized, and costs go up at every step.
  • Dagvaarding / Citation — Court summons. Someone is taking you to court. The paper says when and where the hearing is.
  • Ingebrekestelling / Mise en demeure — Formal demand to pay. The last official warning before court or a bailiff. It often adds interest or a fixed fee.
  • Aanmaning / herinnering — Rappel — Payment reminder. A payment is late. Reminders often add costs or interest if you wait longer.
  • Terugvordering / Récupération de l’indu — Request to pay money back. They say they paid you too much, for example unemployment, sickness or child benefits, and want the money back.
  • Verkeersboete — Onmiddellijke inning / Perception immédiate — Traffic fine. A fine for a traffic offence, often from a speed camera. Pay by the date in the letter to close the case.
  • GAS-boete / Amende administrative (SAC) — Town hall fine. A fine from the town hall for things like parking, litter or noise (municipal administrative sanction).
  • Aanslagbiljet / Avertissement-extrait de rôle — Tax bill. Your tax bill for one year. It shows if you must pay, or get money back.
  • Beslissing / Décision — Decision. A decision about your benefits, residence or another request — for example what you get, a change or a sanction.
  • Bevel om het grondgebied te verlaten / Ordre de quitter le territoire — Order to leave the country. The immigration office says you must leave Belgium by a set date. You can usually appeal, but the deadline is short.
  • Weigering / Refus — Refusal. Your request was refused. The letter says if and how you can challenge it, and by when.
  • Toekenning / Octroi — Approval. Your request was approved. Check that the amounts and dates are right.
  • Uitnodiging / Convocation — Appointment. They want you to come to an appointment, for example at the town hall, the job centre or a doctor. Missing it can cut your benefits or delay your case.
  • Vraag om documenten / Demande de documents — Request for documents. They need papers or information before they can decide, or to keep paying you.
  • Opzegging / Congé — End of contract. Someone ends a contract with you — for example your landlord ends the rental contract.
  • Afrekening / Décompte — Energy or water bill. A bill for electricity, gas or water. The yearly bill compares what you used with your advance payments.

Questions

How long do I have to answer an official letter in Belgium?

It depends on the letter. Look for words like “termijn”, “uiterlijk op”, “délai”, “au plus tard le” — they mark the deadline. In Belgium, a letter sent by post is usually treated as received on the third working day after the date it was sent. Many deadlines, for example 6 months to object to a tax bill, start from that day. To object, the letter usually names the way to do it (“bezwaarschrift”, “beroep”, “verzet”, “bezwaar”).
